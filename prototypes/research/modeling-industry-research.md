# Modeling and Talent Industry Research for Pholio

**Research date:** July 12, 2026  
**Scope:** Fashion/commercial modeling and adjacent casting workflows, with separate attention to aspiring, independent, and represented talent.  
**Purpose:** Establish evidence-based positioning and language for a talent-facing Pholio landing page. This is not a product audit and makes no claim that Pholio currently implements every opportunity identified here.

## Executive conclusion

The modeling career is not primarily a “portfolio problem.” It is a **professional-readiness and continuity problem**.

A model has to maintain several versions of the truth at once: natural digitals that show the current person, a selective book that shows range and proof, a compact comp card that can be reviewed or forwarded quickly, accurate measurements, a public or agency presence, and role-specific submission media. These assets then move through agency applications, castings, callbacks, requests, holds, options, availability, and bookings. The work is visual, but the career is operational.

The category is split across products that solve only one layer:

- agency websites present represented talent beautifully;
- casting marketplaces distribute opportunities and process applications;
- generic site builders publish galleries;
- comp-card makers create an artifact;
- social platforms generate visibility but do not enforce industry readiness;
- agencies and bookers coordinate the represented career, often through systems the model does not fully see.

That leaves a credible territory for Pholio: **the talent-owned professional layer that turns a changing body of work into current, industry-readable materials, then keeps those materials connected to applications and career signals.** The emotional transformation is not “become famous” or “get booked.” It is **from improvising your professionalism to carrying it with you**.

The recommended landing-page territory is:

> **Be ready when the room opens.**  
> Keep your digitals, book, stats, comp card, and submissions current in one professional system built around how modeling actually moves.

This is stronger than “portfolio builder,” which undersells the workflow, and safer than “get discovered” or “book more work,” which implies outcomes no software can guarantee.

## Method and evidence limits

Research prioritized primary or authoritative sources:

- active agency submission pages and public model profiles;
- official support documentation from casting platforms;
- government consumer-protection and labor guidance;
- an industry trade-association glossary;
- current product pages from model-specific and generic portfolio tools;
- a small sample of current personal model sites.

Product and agency pages describe their own systems and may contain promotional claims. Their feature descriptions and submission requirements are useful evidence of workflow; their performance claims are not treated as independently validated. The research did not include private agency software, interviews with models/bookers, or a statistically representative talent survey. Findings labeled **inference** are reasoned from observed workflows rather than directly reported by talent. Before publishing strong quantitative or comparative claims, Pholio should validate them with users and product telemetry.

## 1. How the industry actually reads talent

### The professional identity is modular, not a single gallery

The public profile of an established model demonstrates a clear information hierarchy. IMG’s model pages place the person’s name and basic stats beside a downloadable PDF, then separate material into categories such as editorial, campaigns, tests, runway, covers, and video. [Maya Stepper’s IMG profile](https://imgmodels.com/directory/paris/maya-stepper/) and [Georgia Palmer’s IMG profile](https://imgmodels.com/directory/paris/georgia-palmer/) are examples. Ford similarly separates a model’s **Portfolio**, **Digitals**, and printable material on [Nanne Groenewegen’s profile](https://fordmodels.com/new-york/profile/nanne-groenewegen/portfolio).

That structure is meaningful:

- **Digitals / polaroids** answer “What do you look like now, without production?”
- **The book / portfolio** answers “What have you done, what can you become on camera, and what range can you credibly show?”
- **The comp card / composite / Z-card** answers “Can I assess and forward the essentials quickly?”
- **Tearsheets, campaigns, covers, runway and credits** answer “What is the proof and context of the work?”
- **Video** answers “How do you move and hold attention over time?”
- **Stats and contact/representation** answer “Do you fit the brief, and who can make the booking happen?”

The British Fashion Model Agents Association defines a **book** as the portfolio, now often digital; a **booker** as the agent guiding the career; a **book out** as declared unavailability; and a **buyout** as compensation for specified use, duration, territory and medium. Its [model glossary](https://bfma.fashion/model-glossary/) is a useful source for authentic terminology.

### Natural currentness comes before polish for new faces

Agency applications consistently reject the assumption that an aspiring model first needs an expensive editorial portfolio. ONE Management says smartphone images in natural light, a plain background, fitted simple clothing, no makeup and no poses are sufficient; it also asks for a short natural walking video and personality video. [ONE’s submission FAQ](https://onemanagement.com/submissions/faq) explicitly says professional images are unnecessary. The [Wilhelmina application](https://www.wilhelmina.com/become-a-model) asks applicants to submit images and structured personal information. The [IMG scouting page](https://getscouted.imgmodels.com/) emphasizes safe, verified submission and states that IMG does not request nude/lingerie images or payment.

This has two implications:

1. Pholio should not tell beginners that legitimacy comes from looking expensively produced. Agencies initially want an accurate read, not an imitation campaign.
2. “Professional” should mean **correct, current, clear, complete, and easy to review**—not merely glossy.

The emotional problem for an aspiring model is therefore not simply “I lack photos.” It is: **I do not know what counts, whether I am doing this correctly, or whether the person receiving it is legitimate.**

### The comp card is a decision surface, not a decorative deliverable

ModelManagement’s education material defines a comp card as a small selection—usually three to five representative images—plus name, measurements and contact information. It also says representatives commonly present the comp card rather than the complete portfolio when proposing a model for a role. See its [comp-card chapter](https://www.modelmanagement.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Capitulo-2-Model-Academy_compressed.pdf).

Current model-specific products reinforce the continuing need for portable materials. [Runway Circle](https://runwaycircle.com/) offers auto-generated PDF comp cards and portfolio booklets with a QR code. [Model Folio](https://modelfolio.net/) centers portfolio sites, comp-card conventions and roster management. Current personal sites such as [Devin Riley’s](https://www.thedevinriley.com/) expose portfolio, current digitals, stats, comp card and contact as distinct entry points.

The important marketing point is not “make a beautiful card.” It is:

> **Turn the right images and current facts into the format the next person can actually use.**

### Selection is fast and comparative

Casting-side tools show the receiving context. Backstage’s casting documentation describes applicant cards where reviewers can enlarge a headshot, flip through submitted media, open a detailed profile, review reels and credits, leave notes, place talent into lists, request a self-tape, message them, or move them into shortlist/no states. See [Backstage’s casting-director guide](https://faq.backstage.com/user-guide-casting-director). Casting Networks describes reviewers sorting submissions, watching reels and marking talent **Consider, Hold, or Pass**, then requesting self-tapes and scheduling auditions in its [film and TV workflow](https://support.castingnetworks.com/en/articles/11367617).

This means presentation is evaluated in a queue, not in isolation. The primary design challenge is **clarity under brief attention**, followed by depth when interest increases.

It would be unsafe to claim a universal number of seconds for review without direct study. The evidence supports the qualitative claim that industry systems are designed for rapid scanning and comparison.

## 2. Career journey by talent segment

### Aspiring / unsigned talent

Typical path:

1. Learn whether their market and division may fit them.
2. Produce current digitals and basic measurements.
3. Find official agency submission channels or legitimate open calls.
4. Re-enter similar identity, measurement and media data into multiple forms.
5. Wait, often without a response.
6. If invited, attend a meeting, open call, casting, or provide movement/personality media.
7. Begin development through tests and a progressively stronger book.

ONE notes that because of submission volume it cannot answer every inquiry and only interested applicants should expect a response. That silence is structurally important: it leaves talent uncertain without meaning rejection necessarily reflects a fixable presentation problem. Pholio must not imply its analytics can explain every agency decision.

What this segment cares about:

- Knowing exactly what materials are expected.
- Avoiding an embarrassing or nonstandard submission.
- Looking serious without pretending to have credits they do not have.
- Sharing one clean source of truth rather than improvised folders and attachments.
- Understanding whether materials are current and complete.
- Recognizing legitimate channels and maintaining control over sensitive images.

Emotional needs:

- legitimacy: “I can enter this industry without feeling like an impostor”;
- reassurance: “I am following real standards”;
- agency: “I can prepare even while the outcome is uncertain”;
- safety: “I know what I shared, with whom, and why.”

Messaging that fits:

- “Start with what agencies actually ask to see.”
- “Your current look. Your real measurements. Your work, in the right order.”
- “Build the materials before the opportunity arrives.”

Messaging that does not fit:

- “Get signed faster.”
- “AI finds your perfect agency.”
- “Turn selfies into a modeling career.”
- “Guaranteed discovery.”

### Independent / freelance talent

Typical path:

1. Maintain their own public presence, portfolio, digitals, stats, and contact route.
2. Search marketplaces or receive direct inquiries.
3. Evaluate legitimacy, brief, rate, usage, location and dates.
4. Tailor media and apply.
5. Respond to requests and coordinate availability.
6. Track commitments, work, deliverables and portfolio updates independently.

[ModelManagement’s Model Now page](https://www.modelmanagement.com/apps/) promises worldwide casting search, applications, selection notifications and messages. [Casting Frontier](https://castingfrontier.com/how-it-works-2/) centers searchable talent profiles, role discovery, headshots/video, notifications and direct submission. These are opportunity networks, whereas a generic site builder such as [Wix’s model portfolio product](https://www.wix.com/portfolio-website/industry/model) centers templates, galleries, a domain, SEO and general business tools.

The independent model has the greatest ownership and the greatest administrative burden. A useful Pholio promise is not simply “find castings”; large networks already compete on volume. It is **arrive at each legitimate opportunity with the correct, current package and retain a coherent career record across channels.**

### Represented / working talent

Typical path:

1. Keep booker informed about current stats, look, location and book-outs.
2. Refresh digitals and edit the book as the look and market change.
3. Receive castings, requests, go-sees, options/holds, callbacks and bookings.
4. Supply specific media or information under a deadline.
5. Attend fittings and jobs; understand deal terms, usage and buyouts.
6. Add selected tests, editorials, campaigns, tearsheets and video back to the book.
7. Coordinate multiple agencies or markets where applicable.

Casting Networks’ [talent request tracker](https://support.castingnetworks.com/en/articles/11229057) shows the operational reality: a central alerts inbox for callback, audition, media and question requests; submission history; and stages such as **Submitted**, **Selected**, and **Under Consideration**. Its [mobile app documentation](https://support.castingnetworks.com/en/articles/11228673) describes responding to availability questions and supplying requested photos, video, audio or documents. Its casting-side docs allow up to ten files in a media request and attach deadlines and instructions. These are not merely gallery interactions; they are time-sensitive handoffs.

For working talent, professionalism means:

- nothing stale when a booker or client looks;
- no confusion over which photo, card or measurement is current;
- rapid response without rebuilding the package;
- separation of public material from private or role-specific material;
- a record of applications, attention and next steps that reduces mental overhead.

Messaging that fits:

- “When your look changes, your materials should move with it.”
- “One current book. Every format ready.”
- “Know what is current, what was sent, and what comes next.”

## 3. What makes talent look professional

Across agency, casting and personal-site evidence, the strongest signals are:

1. **Currentness.** Images and measurements represent the person who will arrive.
2. **Correct category.** Digitals are not mixed indistinguishably with campaign work; motion is not buried among stills.
3. **Selection.** The book is edited, not an exhaustive camera roll. The comp card is deliberately compact.
4. **Range with coherence.** Different viable reads are visible without creating a confused identity.
5. **Truthful context.** Credits, publications, clients and representation are accurate rather than inflated.
6. **Industry-readable facts.** Location, measurements, categories, availability/contact and representation are easy to find.
7. **Fast delivery.** Materials can be viewed and forwarded without permissions errors, downloads, oversized attachments or assembly work.
8. **Responsive follow-through.** Requests and deadlines do not disappear across inboxes.
9. **Control and consent.** Sensitive images and personal data are shared deliberately.
10. **No outcome theater.** The presentation is confident without pretending a portfolio guarantees a booking.

These signals suggest the landing page should dramatize a transformation in state: **scattered → classified → edited → formatted → sent → understood → kept current.** It should not dramatize a fantasy transformation from unknown to supermodel.

## 4. Frictions and insecurities

### Evidenced frictions

**Repeated and differing submission requirements.** Agency forms request overlapping but nonidentical combinations of name, location, age, contact, social link, measurements, images and sometimes walking/personality video. Compare [Wilhelmina](https://www.wilhelmina.com/become-a-model), [ONE](https://onemanagement.com/submissions/faq), and [Ford’s current casting form](https://casting-india.fordmodels.com/).

**Multiple media contexts.** Agency profiles separate portfolio categories; casting platforms accept role-specific photos/video/audio/PDFs; personal sites expose digitals, stats, comp cards and contact. The same work must be repackaged for different readers.

**Silence and limited visibility.** ONE states it cannot respond to every application. Casting Networks sells tracking states as a premium capability, demonstrating that knowing whether a submission progressed has value, while also noting that agent-submitted project information may remain with the representative.

**Freshness burden.** Agency standards ask to see the current natural person. Casting advice consistently warns against outdated media. The professional identity is perishable.

**Safety and trust risk.** The FTC warns that fake agents may demand payment, impose photographers, promise work or rush contracts. IMG, ONE and other agencies publish impersonation warnings. This is not peripheral anxiety; it is prominent enough to occupy official submission experiences. See the [FTC modeling-scam guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/modeling-scams).

**Rights and deal complexity.** The BFMA distinguishes day rate and buyout by use, time and territory. New York’s Fashion Workers Act now requires deal memos, transparency around deductions, limits commissions, and requires separate informed approval for digital replicas. See the [New York Department of Labor’s responsibilities summary](https://dol.ny.gov/node/58876). This indicates a broader movement toward talent control and record clarity, though Pholio should not imply legal-compliance functionality unless it exists and has been reviewed.

### Strong inferences to validate with interviews

The following are likely, but not directly measured in this research:

- Models lose time deciding which images constitute “the right” submission rather than uploading itself.
- Independent talent maintains parallel versions across Instagram, Drive/Dropbox, a personal site, casting profiles, PDFs and agency pages.
- A stale measurement or old comp card creates both practical error and fear of looking unserious.
- Talent experiences an asymmetry: they invest heavily in the submission but often see little of the review process.
- Professionals care less about expressive website customization than beginners expect; they care more about image order, currentness, access, forwarding and correctness.
- “AI” is not inherently aspirational in a body- and image-sensitive industry. Intelligence earns trust when it organizes, checks, surfaces or recommends; it loses trust when it alters identity, invents measurements, ranks attractiveness or claims to predict a career.

These should be tested directly with aspiring models, freelance commercial talent, represented new faces, established models, and bookers.

## 5. Competitive landscape and what each category misses

| Category | Core job | Strength | Structural limitation / opening for Pholio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency roster/profile (IMG, Ford) | Present represented talent to clients | Highest visual legitimacy; professional categorization; PDF/print handoff | Controlled by agency; usually begins after representation; not a talent-owned cross-market workspace |
| Casting network (Casting Networks, Backstage, Casting Frontier) | Match roles, receive submissions, process requests | Opportunity inventory; casting-side workflow; alerts and status | Profile is native to that marketplace; media and history can fragment across networks |
| Modeling marketplace (ModelManagement) | Profiles, worldwide castings, applications and messaging | Model-specific audience and terminology | Marketplace-centered; incentives emphasize opportunities and membership rather than a neutral professional source of truth |
| Generic website builder (Wix, Squarespace-like tools) | Publish a branded web presence | Design flexibility, domain, SEO, general business tooling | Requires talent to know industry structure; website is not naturally connected to submission lifecycle or comp-card logic |
| Model portfolio/comp-card builder (Model Folio, Runway Circle) | Generate industry-shaped public presence and artifacts | Faster setup; model-specific templates; PDF comp cards | Often artifact-centric; limited career intelligence or application continuity |
| Social profile / shared folder | Distribute work quickly | Familiar, low friction, broad reach | Weak curation, unstable order/context, permission errors, no professional completeness model, limited privacy granularity |
| Agency/booker operations | Represent, pitch, coordinate and negotiate | Human judgment, access, relationships and accountability | Not available to unsigned talent; model visibility into the workflow varies; should not be impersonated by software |

The whitespace is not “all of these features in one app.” That language sounds like generic SaaS and invites a feature-comparison battle. The whitespace is a distinct role:

> **Pholio is the talent’s professional source of truth—the place where work becomes current materials, and current materials become career movement.**

## 6. Authentic industry language

Use terms because they shorten and clarify real work, not as fashion decoration.

| Term | Practical meaning | Landing-page use |
|---|---|---|
| **Book** | The edited portfolio, physical or digital | Prefer over repeatedly saying “content library” |
| **Digitals / polaroids** | Natural, minimally produced current images | Essential; consider “digitals” primary and explain once |
| **Comp card / composite** | Portable selection of images, stats and contact/agency | Essential and specific |
| **Test** | Shoot undertaken to develop material, relationships or a look | Useful when explaining book growth |
| **Tearsheet** | Published work as it appeared in a publication | Use if Pholio supports it |
| **Booker** | Agency professional managing/pitching talent | Use carefully; do not claim Pholio replaces one |
| **Board** | An agency’s category/roster grouping | Useful in agency-facing submission context |
| **Casting** | Selection process for a specific assignment | Standard |
| **Open call** | Open agency/client evaluation without individual appointment | Standard if supported |
| **Go-see** | Meeting/presentation to a client or industry contact | Strong industry signal if it maps to product states |
| **Callback / recall** | Further selection stage after initial review | Standard |
| **Hold / option** | Tentative reservation or priority against dates | Only use if the product accurately represents these distinctions |
| **Book out** | Mark a period unavailable | Strong operational language if availability is supported |
| **Booking** | Confirmed job/assignment | Never use “bookings” to describe mere inquiries |
| **Usage / buyout** | Rights, media, term, territory and compensation for use of likeness | Important trust territory; legal review required for claims |
| **New face / development** | Early-stage agency talent being developed | More authentic than “beginner creator” |

### Language that sounds native

- current digitals
- edit your book
- submission-ready
- the card your work deserves
- selected work
- your stats, current
- private until you send it
- built for a booker’s read
- know what was sent
- ready for the next request
- from first digitals to a working book
- one link that always reflects the current you

### Startup language to avoid

- all-in-one platform
- creator ecosystem
- unlock your potential
- elevate your career
- revolutionize your portfolio
- AI-powered personal brand
- seamless end-to-end solution
- get discovered by top agencies
- book more jobs
- stand out from the crowd
- data-driven success
- optimize your look
- democratizing fashion
- your career, reimagined

The problem with these phrases is not only cliché. They obscure who does what, imply causation the product cannot prove, and sound alien beside the concrete nouns the industry uses.

## 7. Positioning territories

### Territory A — “Industry-ready from the start”

**Transformation:** uncertainty and improvised materials → correct, complete, credible presentation.

**Best for:** aspiring models and new faces.

**Proof language:** digitals, stats, book structure, comp card, submission package, readiness checks.

**Emotional payoff:** legitimacy and calm.

**Risk:** can make Pholio feel like an educational onboarding tool that professionals outgrow.

**Possible line:** “Start with what the industry actually needs to see.”

### Territory B — “The living book”

**Transformation:** static portfolio → current, evolving career identity.

**Best for:** broadest visual storytelling and premium fashion tone.

**Proof language:** classify work, keep digitals separate, edit by category, update once, generate cards and public presentation.

**Emotional payoff:** authorship and control.

**Risk:** “living portfolio” is relatively easy for generic website builders to imitate; it can underplay applications and intelligence.

**Possible line:** “Your book should move as fast as your career does.”

### Territory C — “Ready before the room; informed after it”

**Transformation:** unseen preparation and opaque waiting → professional package and legible movement.

**Best for:** Pholio’s combined presentation, submission and intelligence story; strong cinematic narrative.

**Proof language:** assemble the right package, send current materials, track real states/qualified attention, surface next actions.

**Emotional payoff:** preparedness before judgment and agency after submission.

**Risk:** claims about attention and agency identity must be tightly grounded in what Pholio actually measures. It cannot imply knowledge of private deliberations.

**Possible line:** “Be ready when the room opens. Know what moves after you leave it.”

### Territory D — “Talent-owned career infrastructure”

**Transformation:** career scattered across agency pages, casting profiles, folders and PDFs → one source of truth the talent controls.

**Best for:** represented and independent professionals; defensible category creation.

**Proof language:** version control for materials, privacy, rights/credits, availability, representation, submission history.

**Emotional payoff:** sovereignty and durability.

**Risk:** “infrastructure” and “operating system” can feel technical and emotionally cold if used as hero copy. Better as strategic framing than the first line.

**Possible line:** “Your work changes. Your representation changes. Your professional record stays yours.”

### Territory E — “Intelligence that handles the work around the work”

**Transformation:** manual sorting and uncertainty → assisted organization, freshness and next-action clarity.

**Best for:** product differentiation once trust has been established.

**Proof language:** image classification, category suggestions, freshness checks, package readiness, material performance and qualified signals—only where implemented.

**Emotional payoff:** relief and focus.

**Risk:** leading with AI invites fear of image manipulation, synthetic identity, attractiveness scoring, and inflated automation claims. New York’s explicit consent rules for digital replicas make careless AI framing especially inappropriate.

**Possible line:** “Intelligence for the decisions around your images—not a replacement for your image.”

## 8. Recommended synthesis

Use **Territory C as the narrative spine**, **Territory B as the visual metaphor**, **Territory D as the category definition**, and **Territory E as a late reveal**.

The combined position:

> Pholio is the talent-owned system that keeps a model’s book, digitals, stats, cards, and submissions current—so they can enter each opportunity prepared and understand the real movement that follows.

Why this combination wins:

- It serves both aspiring and working talent without pretending their needs are identical.
- It begins with a human truth—being judged in rooms you may not enter—rather than a software category.
- It turns the portfolio into a persistent narrative object that can transform into digitals, book categories, comp card and dossier.
- It gives intelligence a purpose after the materials have entered the market.
- It avoids claiming that software replaces the taste, relationships, negotiation or duty of a booker.
- It supports precise, demonstrable claims instead of “all-in-one.”

### Landing-page message hierarchy

1. **Identity:** You are the work; Pholio is not trying to manufacture a persona.
2. **Tension:** Your career is judged through scattered, perishable versions of you.
3. **Order:** Pholio understands the difference between digitals, book, tests, campaigns, tearsheets and motion.
4. **Artifact:** The work becomes a current, forwardable comp card and professional presence.
5. **Action:** The right material becomes a deliberate submission, not an improvised attachment.
6. **Opacity:** The package enters a review process the talent cannot fully see.
7. **Signal:** Pholio returns only defensible movement, attention or state—not invented certainty.
8. **Continuity:** New work, new stats, availability and representation update the same lasting professional record.
9. **Invitation:** Join to get your materials ready now, not to buy a promised outcome.

### Claim discipline

Safe if supported by the product audit:

- “Keep digitals separate from the work in your book.”
- “Build a comp card from current images and stats.”
- “Choose exactly what enters a submission.”
- “Control which work is public and which stays private.”
- “Keep credits and image context attached to the work.”
- “See documented submission states and qualified attention.”

Unsafe without stronger evidence or functionality:

- “Agencies prefer Pholio.”
- “Get reviewed faster.”
- “Know exactly what agencies think.”
- “See who viewed you” when identity is inferred or anonymous.
- “AI selects the images that will book.”
- “Guaranteed industry-standard portfolio.”
- “Apply to every agency from one place.”
- “Protect your image rights” unless the product provides reviewed rights workflows.

## 9. Trust requirements for Pholio’s marketing

Trust should be a visible design layer, not a footer disclaimer.

- Explain what a signal means and what it does not mean.
- Distinguish verified agency actions from anonymous or inferred activity.
- State that Pholio is not an agency and does not guarantee representation or work.
- Avoid pay-to-access framing that resembles “secure your spot” schemes.
- Make privacy and image-level visibility controls concrete.
- Explain whether uploaded work trains models or is used for generative systems.
- Never imply that intelligence scores beauty, body suitability or career potential.
- Treat minors, sensitive images and personal measurements with heightened care.
- If agency verification exists, explain the standard; if it does not, do not imply every opportunity is vetted.

The FTC says legitimate agencies do not guarantee assignments or salaries and warns against upfront fees to secure work. Pholio’s conversion language should therefore sell **preparedness, organization and agency**, not access to an assured result.

## 10. Validation work before final copy

Run short interviews or concept tests with at least:

- 4 aspiring models who have applied to agencies;
- 4 represented new faces;
- 4 working commercial/editorial models across more than one market;
- 3 independent models who self-submit;
- 3 bookers or agents;
- 2 casting professionals.

Questions to resolve:

1. Which materials exist in how many separate places today?
2. What changed most recently, and how many places had to be updated?
3. What is the last submission or request that created avoidable work?
4. Which status information is genuinely useful versus anxiety-producing?
5. What would make a professional distrust an “intelligent” portfolio product?
6. Does “operating system” feel authoritative or like startup theater?
7. Is “Be ready when the room opens” motivating to professionals as well as aspirants?
8. Which Pholio artifact would a model actually send tomorrow?

Success is not users saying the page looks luxurious. It is users accurately describing Pholio afterward as something close to:

> “It keeps my actual modeling materials current and ready to send, then helps me keep track of what is moving.”
